## Portfolio Return and Volatility
Portfolio weight for $i$-th investment is deterministic (**price-agnostic**)
$$
x_i=\frac{\text{Value of investment i}}{\text{Total value of portfolio}}
$$
Realized return for a portfolio
$$
R_p=x_1R_1+x_2R_2+\cdots+x_nR_n=\sum_ix_iR_i
$$
- $x=(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n)$
- $R=(R_1,R_2,\ldots R_n)'$

Expected return for a portfolio
$$
E[R_p]=x_1E(R_1)+\cdots+x_NE(R_n)=\sum_i x_iE[R_i]
$$
Covariance (TESTABLE)
$$
\begin{align}
Cov(R_1,R_2)&=E[(R_1-E[R_1])(R_2-E[R_2])] \\
&=E[R_1R_2-R(E(R_2)-E(R_1)R_2+E(R_1)E(R_2))] \\
&=E(R_1R_2)-E[R_1E(R_2)]-E[E(R_1)R_2]+E[E(R_1)E(R_2)] \\
&= E[R_1R_2]-E[R_1]E[R_2]-E[R_1]E[R_2]+E[R_1]E[R_2] \\
&=E[R_1R_2]-E[R_1]E[R_2]
\end{align}
$$
Estimating covariance
$$
\begin{align}
Cov(R_i,R_j)&=\frac{1}{T-1}\sum_t (R_{i,t}-\overline{R_i})(R_{j,t}-\overline{R_j}) \\
&=\frac{1}{T-1}[\sum_r(R_{i,t}\times R_{j,t})-T\overline{R_i}\overline{R_j}]
\end{align}
$$
Correlation
$$
Corr(R_i,R_j)= \frac{Cov(R_i,R_j)}{SD(R_i)SD(R_j)}
$$
Volatility of a portfolio with two stocks
$$
\begin{align}
Var(R_p)&=(x_1)^2Var(R_1)+(x_2)^2Var(R_2)+2x_1x_2Corr(R_1,R_2)\sqrt{Var(R_1)Var(R_2)} \\
\end{align}
$$
$$
Volatility = SD(R_p)=\sqrt{Var(R_p)}
$$
## Efficient Portfolios of Risky Assets
A portfolio $P$ is inefficient if there exists another portfolio $Q$ such that either there's both lower risk and lower reward
1. $E[R_Q]>E[R_P]$ AND $SD(R_Q)\leq SD(R_P)$
2. $E[R_Q]\leq E[R_P]$ AND $SD(R_Q)<SD(R_P)$

Shorting
$\sum_i x_i=1$ but it's not necessary that $0\leq x_i\leq 1$

Volatility of a portfolio with $n$ 
$$
Var(R_p)=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^nx_ix_jCov(R_i,R_j)Cov(R_i, R_j)=x'\Omega x
$$
$$
Var(R_p)=Cov(R_p, R_p)=Cov(\sum_{i} x_iR_i, R_p)
$$
Rearranging
$$
\begin{align}
Var(R_p)&= \sum_i Cov(R_i, R_p) \\
&=\sum_i\sum_jx_ix_jCov(R_i, R_j) \\
&=\sum_i x_i^2Var(R_i)+\sum_i\sum_{j, j\neq i}x_ix_jCov(R_i,R_j)
\end{align}
$$
Volatility of an equally weighted portfolio with $n$ securities:
$$
\begin{align}
Var(R_p)&=(\frac{1}{n})\times\text{Avg variance of individual securities} \\
&+(1-\frac{1}{n})\times\text{Avg covaraince between the securities}
\end{align}
$$

p*= \frac{S_0(1+r_f)-S^-_1}{S_1^+-S_1^-} = \frac{1+r_f-d}{u-d}
$$

## The Binomial Model
$$
p*=\frac{\sqrt{1+r_f}-d}{u-d}
$$
Convergence of the Binomial Model
- $nT$ periods of expiration $T$, each of length $h=\frac{1}{n}$ years
- $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of the annual rate of return of the underlying stock, with $u=e^{\sigma\sqrt{h}}, d=\frac{1}{u}$
- The risk-free rate $r$ is assumed to be continuously compounded
- The discount factor over any period is $e^{-rh}$ and the risk-neutral probability of an up move in any period is 
$$
p^*+\frac{e^{rh}-d}{u-d}
$$
- After $n$ periods there are $n+1$ possible stock prices $S_0u^jd^{n-j}$ for $j=0,1,\ldots,n$ with associated risk-neutral probabilities
$$
\binom{n}{j}(p^*)^j(1-p^*)^{n-j}
$$
## The Black-Scholes Model
Can be viewed as the limit of the binomial model as $n\to\infty$
- The basic version applies to European options on stocks which don't pay dividends before $T$
- Assumes changes in underlying stock prices have a **lognormal** distribution (changes in the natural log of the price are normally distributed)

Notation
- Current stock price  = $S_0$
- Expiration time (years) = $T$
- Strike = $K$
- Risk-free interest rate (continuously compounded) = $r$
- Standard deviation (volatility) = $\sigma$

Black-Scholes Formula
$$
\begin{align}
C_0&=S_0N(d_1)-Ke^{-rT}N(d_2) \\
P_0&=Ke^{-rT}N(-d_2)-S_0N(-d_1) \\
d_1&=\frac{\ln[\frac{S_0}{PV(K))}]}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}+\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2} \\
d_2&=d_1-\sigma\sqrt{T}
\end{align}
$$
## Risk and Expected Return for Options
Option betas can be calculated using the replicating portfolio since the option value is $S_0\Delta+B$
$$
\begin{align}
\beta_{option}&=[\frac{S_0\Delta}{S_0\Delta+B}]\times\beta_S + [\frac{S_0\Delta}{S_0\Delta+B}]\times\beta_B \\
&=[\frac{S_0\Delta}{S_0\Delta+B}]\times \beta_S
\end{align}
$$
$$
[\frac{S_0\Delta}{S_0\Delta+B}] = \text{leverage ratio}
$$
Under Black-Scholes,
- Call: $\Delta=N(D_1), B=-Ke^{-rT}N(d_2)$
- Put: $\Delta=-N(-d_1), B=Ke^{-rT}N(-d_2)$

Leverage ratio
- For a call option, $\Delta>0, B<0$, so the leverage ratio $>1$ and $\beta_{option}>\beta_s$
- For a put option, $\Delta<), B>0$, so the leverage ratio $<0$ and $\beta_{option}<0$
- The magnitude of the leverage ratio is higher for out-of-money options

## Forwards
Forward = an agreement to buy or sell an asset at a specified future delivery date at a price set today called the forward price (a.k.a. delivery price)
- **like an option without the option**
- the buying party is long, the selling party is short

How are forward prices determined?
- For financial assets (stocks, bond, currencies) and commodities held by a lot of investors (gold), price is determined by no-arbitrage
- For commodities held by firms for production (copper), price is determined by supply and demand

Buy now and hold VS buy forward for later?
- Depends on **whether the asset pays income** or if it incurs **storage costs** (oil in 2020)
