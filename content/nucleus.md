# Part 1: New Media Strategy

## 30 Day Plan
**Objectives**
1. **Clean everything up:** Public image is everything. I’d clean up the descriptions of X / LinkedIn / Substack / [nucleustalent.io](http://nucleustalent.io) with concise descriptions of the firm and Nucleus’ sleek, modern aesthetic.
2. **Start gaining traction:** After a month, people should start noticing and talking about Nucleus. I predict that at least one of the following pieces of media should attract attention.
3. **Aggressively market the NEXT fellowship:** Applications close on Jan 9th, a few days after I join. I’d make it my first priority to market the fellowship and make sure every smart person I know applies.
4. **Familiarize myself with the workflow:** I’d love to understand how the Nucleus machine churns on a day-to-day basis. I plan to spend a day shadowing each member of the team and understanding what they’d need help with.
5. **Event 1: Dinner**: The first event will be more exclusive and low-key. I’m thinking dinner at a nice restaurant in SF with some of the fellowship mentors and the Nucleus crew.

**Examples of content**
- An X thread + LinkedIn post about the NEXT fellowship
- An X thread + LinkedIn post + Substack Article about the [Nucleus Growth 100](https://nucleustalent.io/nucleus100) List that was broadcast on TBPN
- Clips from The Rise podcast
- Posts and media from the dinner event

**Measurement**
- Quantitatively, we want to maintain high views / impressions / engagement on X / LinkedIn / Substack. We also want to increase fellowship applications (eg, double the # of apps in the last week)
- Quantitatively, we want people to start noticing and talking about Nucleus. We’ll collect feedback on our dinner and any other events.

**Judgment**
- The first month is the time to move fast and experiment a lot
- At the same time, it’s important to be disciplined and selective in regard to what we show the world - i.e, we won’t click post until something is done well

## Detail Areas
**X Native Strategy**
- We’re chasing **trust, not virality**. We don’t need our posts to blow up, but to be consistently seen by the people they’re meant for
- Our tone should be casual but command attention
- Threads are dedicated to long-form posts (eg, Substack article summary), posts are for one-off things (eg, event announcement), and clips are for podcasts / hype videos

**Podcast to Media Loop**
- Moments are worth clipping if they’re insightful, thought-provoking, or entertaining
- There should be a clear separation between clips that are educational and those meant to promote a guest or their firm
- I would do extensive research on the backgrounds of our guests and come up with thought-provoking questions to ask them
- As I discussed with Joseph J, we want questions that allow guests to **stake their claims** on the future. If one of them can predict the next Bitcoin in 2012, every VC firm will see the clip and want to hire them.

**Fellowship, Events, and Community**
- During the first week of the Fellowship, we should do a “Meet The Fellows” series of posts
- The public needs to see what’s going in inside the NEXT fellowship, which is why I’d focus on a weekly/biweekly recap series (“This Week in NEXT”) on what the fellows are up to
- There should be media (photos, videos) whenever there’s a fellowship event to build hype
- Our events should still focus on bringing together the 4 types of people (managers, rising stars, builders, and LPs)

**Substack and Long Form**
- Substack is thorough, long-form content for those who see our X page and want more
- Long-form content should also include graphics (eg, the [Nucleus Gravity Index](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/G0GDgnLaQAAZM7N?format=jpg&name=4096x4096) )
- I [write on Substack myself](https://zhaju.substack.com/), so I’d love to write a piece or two for Nucleus
